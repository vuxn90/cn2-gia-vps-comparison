## What makes a CN2 GIA VPS different?

CN2 GIA is China Telecom’s premium international routing network, identified with AS4809. It is designed to provide more stable China-to-international connectivity than ordinary ChinaNet routes, commonly identified with AS4134.

That distinction matters because regular routes can experience congestion during busy periods. BandwagonHost’s own network explanation separates ChinaNet, CN2 GT, CN2 GIA, and CTGNet, describing CN2 GIA as the most stable but also the most expensive and capacity-constrained option.

A VPS advertised as “CN2” should therefore be checked carefully. There is a meaningful difference between:

- **CN2 GT**, which may still suffer congestion.
- **CN2 GIA**, which is the premium AS4809 route.
- **CTGNet**, identified with AS23764.
- **Multi-carrier China connectivity**, where China Telecom, China Unicom, and China Mobile are all included.

BandwagonHost states that its Los Angeles USCA_9 data center sends China-bound traffic through China Telecom CN2 GIA, China Mobile CMIN2, and China Unicom Premium. Its Hong Kong and Japan plans also advertise direct or preferred routes through major Chinese carriers.

This does not mean every user will see the same latency from every Chinese province or ISP. China Telecom, China Unicom, and China Mobile can produce different results, and routing can change over time. A premium route improves the odds of consistent performance; it is not a magic spell that defeats geography, congestion, or every network-policy problem.

## CN2 GIA limitations you should understand

CN2 GIA is not the same thing as guaranteed performance for every type of traffic.

First, routing depends on the user’s ISP and location. A visitor using China Telecom may take a different path from a China Mobile user. Second, premium routing does not automatically solve DNS problems, application bottlenecks, database latency, or international content restrictions. Third, BandwagonHost warns that CN2 GIA capacity is limited and that this network is not tolerant of large DDoS attacks. The provider says it may use IP nullrouting during attacks.

There is also an important difference between bandwidth capacity and bandwidth allowance. A plan may advertise a 2.5 Gbps or 10 Gbps link while still imposing a monthly transfer limit. A high port speed does not mean unlimited monthly traffic.

Before ordering, check:

1. The actual data center code.
2. The inbound and outbound routes.
3. Monthly transfer allowance.
4. Billing cycle and renewal price.
5. Refund and SLA terms.
6. Whether the location can be migrated later.
7. Whether the plan is in stock at checkout.
8. Whether your intended use complies with the provider’s terms.
